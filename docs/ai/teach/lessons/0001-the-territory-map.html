The Territory Map

Before you can re-architect this repo, you need to see it whole. The trick: it's two arcs that meet at one place — the spec coming down, the circuit coming up, joined at the per-opcode equivalence theorems. Everything else hangs off that.

Your win today — draw the repo from memory as two converging arcs, name each territory and its job, and say in one sentence what a green lake build actually proves.

1The one shape to hold in your head

The whole project exists to prove one statement, 63 times over (once per RV64IM opcode) and then aggregate them:

Sail spec (what RISC-V means)  =  ZisK circuit (what the zkVM does)

The left-hand side is generated from the official spec. The right-hand side is generated from ZisK's real circuit constraints. The proof tower in the middle is the work of showing they agree. Read the map below top-to-bottom: generation first (how the two sides get into Lean), then the tower (how they're proved equal).

2What each territory is for

Re-arch lens — Airs/ (op-bus model) and AirsClean/ (Clean-DSL ensemble) are two circuit-side stacks that grew at different times. Whether they should stay separate, merge, or one should subsume the other is one of the sharpest re-architecture questions. We'll dedicate a later lesson to it. The RHS (circuit) — three layers. pil-extract drops raw, generated constraint files into build/extraction/ (gitignored). Airs/ wraps those into human-readable named-column records (Valid_<AIR>) plus the operation-bus model that says how an instruction maps to circuit rows. AirsClean/ formalizes the AIR ensemble in the Clean DSL. ZiskCircuit/ states the circuit's actual arithmetic semantics in BitVec/field terms.

The LHS (spec) — one layer. build/sail-lean/ is the official RISC-V spec compiled to Lean. SailSpec/ is the thin hand-written bridge: per-opcode "pure specs" restating the Sail semantics in a shape the proofs can chew on.

Re-arch lens — the 3-layer tower is the densest, most duplicated region (234 files for 63 opcodes ≈ 3.7 files/opcode). The in-repo simplification-suggestions.md is your ranked backlog here — several collapses already shipped. This is priority #1 for your mission. The tower — where the proof lives. For each opcode: EquivCore/<Op> does the real work (spec = bus effect, given a well-formed AIR row). Compliance/Wrappers/<Op> discharges the row/table facts from the provider's Valid_<AIR> spec — but, as Lesson 02 shows, the per-opcode "promise" bundle is not eliminated; it's relocated upward into the OpEnvelope. Equivalence/<Op> restates the result in the canonical channel-balance form — these 63 files are the public per-opcode theorems. Compliance/Dispatch/ aggregates them per instruction-family, and Compliance.lean routes all 63 through a sum type into the single global theorem.

Supporting cast. Field/ (Goldilocks field + the one global FGL instance), Bits/ (PackedBitVec no-wrap toolkits), Channels/ (the channel-balance machinery), RowShape/, and Tactics/ (proof automation) are shared infrastructure the tower leans on. Completeness/ is a separate coverage claim (every decodable RV64IM word is accepted), backed by the Aeneas extraction of ZisK's Rust lowering.

3What "green" actually means

Two distinct gates, often conflated — plus one subtlety the slogans hide.

1. lake build typechecks ⇒ every per-opcode equiv_<OP> and the global zisk_riscv_compliant_program_bus hold in Lean's kernel. This is the formal-verification claim. Everything else is scaffolding.

Read the signature, not the slogan — verified against Compliance.lean:95. The bridge + no-known-defect hypotheses are the residual trust, expressed as theorem parameters the caller must supply — tracked in the caller-burden ledger, not as axioms. "0 axioms" is true and the trust is real; it just moved. But it is not unconditional. The global theorem's signature is zisk_riscv_compliant_program_bus (env : OpEnvelope) (h_bridge : env.aeneasBridgeTrust) (h_memory_construction) (h_known_bugs : Defects.NoKnownDefect env) : env.exec_eq. In words: for any opcode arm, given Aeneas-bridge trust, memory-timeline construction evidence, and that the arm is outside the ledgered known-defect regions (trust/defects.md), Sail = circuit. So green ≠ "ZisK matches the spec, period." It's "matches, modulo these stated premises and known defects."

2. The trust gate passes ⇒ the shape of that claim hasn't been quietly weakened — no new axioms, no laundered hypotheses, no over-strong validators. It guards the claim's integrity over time, not its truth at one instant.

Mission-critical for re-architecting: the "0 axioms" headline is true, but the residual trust is relocated into the global theorem's hypotheses + the caller-burden ledger — not eliminated. A refactor can keep lake build green while silently moving trust around; the trust gate exists to catch that. Respect it, don't route around it.
