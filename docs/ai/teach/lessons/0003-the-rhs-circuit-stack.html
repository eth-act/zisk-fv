The RHS Circuit Stack

In Lesson 02, the ADD proof bottomed out at "a well-formed BinaryRow from a valid provider table." Today: where does that row come from, and why are there three different in-tree representations of the same ZisK circuit? This is the most layered and most duplicated territory in the repo — which makes it the richest re-architecture ground.

Your win today — name the three circuit-side trees, say what each is for and at what granularity, and point to the exact seam (and the literal duplication) a re-architecture would target.
From Lesson 02: the RHS endpoint was a BinaryRow + an Air.Flat.Table whose spec proved the row well-formed. Those names come from AirsClean; the bus/Valid_ machinery comes from Airs. Today we see how they fit.

1Generation: pilout → pil-extract → two targets

The RHS starts as ZisK's real constraints, compiled to a protobuf (build/zisk.pilout, 22 AIRs — ~10 on the RV64IM path). The Rust tool tools/pil-extract reads it. The architecturally important fact: it has two different emission targets, born at different times.

So even the extractor carries a legacy/modern split: the air path predates the Clean migration and lingers (Mem cross-check only); clean-component is the current target. docs/extraction/extractor-notes.md · lakefile.toml (per-AIR extraction "outside the main Lake dependency graph")

2The three hand-stewarded trees

Downstream of generation sit three Lean trees. The trap is thinking they're three layers of one stack — they aren't. They model the circuit at two different granularities and partly overlap.

ZiskFv/Airs/32 files · per AIR-family
Named-column Valid_<AIR> records, the operation-bus & memory-bus models (matches_entry, opBus_row_*), the lookup tables (BinaryTable, …), and "packed-correct" BitVec lemmas.
Role: the model everything downstream consumes. Despite the Clean migration, this is still the spine.
ZiskFv/AirsClean/73 files · per AIR-family
The Clean-DSL components. Per family ~6 files: Row/Constraints (the generated, faithful-by-construction pair) + Circuit/Spec/Soundness/Bridge (hand-written proofs). FullEnsemble assembles them.
Role: the modern, generation-anchored circuit surface — validated AIR-by-AIR (BinaryAdd, MemAlignByte, …).
ZiskFv/ZiskCircuit/65 files · per OPCODE
Per-opcode compositional circuit-correctness in pure field arithmetic. E.g. Add.lean: given Main's ADD-subset constraints + BinaryAdd's carry chain + the op-bus match, prove the Main row's c lanes = the 64-bit sum of a+b.
Role: "the circuit computes the right number for this opcode." Built on Airs; consumed by EquivCore.

Granularity tell — Airs and AirsClean are indexed by AIR family (Binary, Arith, Mem…). ZiskCircuit is indexed by opcode (Add, Sub, Lw…). That mismatch is why a single opcode proof pulls from all three: ZiskCircuit/<Op> for the arithmetic, Airs for the bus/table, AirsClean for the row. Note the granularity: Airs and AirsClean are per-AIR; ZiskCircuit is per-opcode. And note the dependency direction — ZiskCircuit/Add.lean imports Airs (Main, BinaryAdd, OperationBus), not AirsClean. The arithmetic-correctness layer was built on the older model.

3The seam — and the duplication

Here's the payoff, and the thing a re-architecture has to confront. AirsClean does not replace Airs. It bridges into it. A generated Clean BinaryRow is lowered into the legacy Valid_Binary record that the bus model and EquivCore already understand:

the bridge that joins the two stacks
AirsClean.Binary.validOfRow : BinaryRow FGL → Airs.…Valid_Binary FGL FGL

Its docstring: "Valid_Binary ↔ BinaryRow compatibility … No Circuit.main left." It even reconstructs "the eight legacy BinaryTable.wf_properties facts." So the modern row is translated back into the legacy shape before the proof proceeds.

ZiskFv/AirsClean/Binary/Bridge.lean:346 · used in EquivCore/<Op> and Compliance/Wrappers/<Op>

Re-arch lens — three questions this territory forces: (1) Should Airs's hand-written Valid_ model be subsumed by generated AirsClean, removing the validOfRow bridge? (2) Should the duplicated tables be unified to one source of truth? (3) Can the legacy air→OpenVM-shim extraction be retired once Mem moves to clean-component? Each shrinks the RHS surface; none is free. And the duplication is not subtle. The Binary operation table is defined twice, in two namespaces, each with its own opcode constants:

ZiskFv.Airs.Tables.BinaryTable  (OP_LTU=0x06, …)   vs   ZiskFv.AirsClean.BinaryTable  (tableSize, OP_MINU=0x02, …)

Two definitions of the same upstream table is exactly the kind of accreted seam your mission is about: harmless to the proof (both are checked), but a real cost to comprehension and change. ZiskFv/Airs/Tables/BinaryTable.lean:29 · ZiskFv/AirsClean/BinaryTable.lean:28

4One honest aside: where constructibility lives

The split matters for trust, not just tidiness. The generated AirsClean Row/Constraints are faithful-by-construction — their fidelity is the extractor's job, audited by diff. But the hand-written Airs Valid_<AIR> records are where the constructibility risk sits: if a Valid_ record declares constraints stronger than ZisK's circuit enforces, every equivalence theorem over it becomes vacuous (you can't build the instance). That's why the trust gate's anti-laundering rules single out Valid_<AIR> changes. The validOfRow bridge is the project's current answer: tie the hand-written record to a generated row so the two can't drift apart silently.
