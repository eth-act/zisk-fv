One Opcode, End to End

Lesson 01 ended on a warning: the global theorem is conditioned, not absolute. Today we make that concrete by following a single opcode — ADD — all the way up the tower, watching exactly where each hypothesis is born, where it gets discharged, and where it simply relocates. By the end you'll know precisely what this project proves about ADD and what it assumes.

Your win today — for one real opcode, separate what the math proves from what the OpEnvelope assumes, and name the layer that does each job.

Re-arch lens — notice the burden never disappears; it moves. Rungs 1–3 are real reductions (arithmetic ← row ← table). Rungs 4–6 are plumbing + relocation. A re-architecture question worth holding: should the per-opcode promises be derived (from the extraction/trace side) rather than parked as OpEnvelope fields a caller must hand-build? That single decision reshapes the top half of this tower. The headline: rungs 1–3 genuinely discharge (arithmetic from a row, row from a table). Rungs 4–6 don't discharge anything — rung 4 reshapes, rung 5 unpacks, rung 6 aggregates and bolts on the three global premises. The per-opcode promises ride all the way up and end their life as constructor fields of the OpEnvelope you must build.

3So what is actually proven — and what is assumed?

This is the question the mission turns on. For ADD, validated against source:

✓ Proven (the math)
  • Given a well-formed Binary row, its byte/carry arithmetic equals Sail's ADD.
  • Given a valid provider table, such a row exists and is well-formed.
  • The two conclusion forms (bus_effect.2 ↔ channel-balance) coincide.
✗ Assumed (bundled into env / global)
  • promises : RTypePromises — the Sail-state ↔ bus correspondence (§4).
  • h_match — the op-bus permutation lines up; pins — the Main row.
  • aeneasBridgeTrust, memoryTimelineConstruction, NoKnownDefect.

This is why "0 axioms" and "real residual trust" are both true at once. Nothing is an axiom; the trust is the right-hand column — obligations you must satisfy to construct an OpEnvelope and supply the three globals. The caller-burden ledger is exactly the catalogue of that right-hand column.

4What the hypotheses mean: RTypePromises

You asked what these hypotheses actually say. Here's the per-opcode bundle for ADD, in plain terms — 15 structural facts asserting the circuit's bus rows faithfully mirror what Sail did:

register reads
input_r1_eq, input_r2_eq — reading rs1/rs2 in Sail yields exactly the values the row consumed.
target & pc
input_rd_eq, input_pc_eq, nextPC_matches — rd index, current PC, and next PC line up with the spec.
exec-bus shape
exec_len=2, e0_mult=-1 (consume), e1_mult=1 (produce).
mem-bus roles
m0/m1_mult=-1 reads, m2_mult=1 write; all as=1 (register space); rd_idx matches e2's target.

The honest core — these promises are the bridge between two models (Sail's state monad and the bus) that the arithmetic proof can't see. Until something derives them (the Aeneas/trace side), they are assumed. That's not a flaw to hide — it's the precise location of the remaining work, and the thing a re-architecture must either preserve or close. None of these are proved in the ADD tower — they're the premises the arithmetic proof needs in order to connect "Sail read register rs1" to "the circuit row's input lane A." They are supplied when the .add_via_binary envelope is constructed. ZiskFv/EquivCore/Promises/RType.lean:31 (structure, 15 fields) · field of OpEnvelope.add_via_binary at OpEnvelope.lean:391
